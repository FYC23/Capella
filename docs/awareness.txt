Personal stories shared through platforms like My Stuttering Life (https://www.mystutteringlife.com/), StutterTalk (https://stuttertalk.com/) ,The Stuttering Society (https://www.stutteringsociety.com/category/stories/) and SAY’s (https://say.org/mystutterblog/) storytelling projects show stuttering not as a weakness, but a part of many people’s everyday lives. Famous people who stutter
Emily Blunt: Actor
Emily Blunt experienced stuttering throughout her childhood and has spoken about how it shaped her confidence. She says that acting and adopting different voices helped her find new ways of speaking. 

James Earl Jones: Actor & Voice Icon
James Earl Jones with his deep and resonant voice (including the voice of Darth Vader), grew up with a severe stutter. He worked with supportive teachers and found comfort in poetry and reading aloud. 

President Joe Biden
Joe Biden has spoken publicly about stuttering since childhood, sharing stories of practicing speeches in front of the mirror. 

Samuel L. Jackson: Actor
Samuel L. Jackson has mentioned his stutter in interviews and explained how he surpassed his insecurity by performing and gaining supporters for his condition. 


Culture Attitudes 
Western Countries (e.g., U.S., Canada, U.K.)
In many Western countries, public awareness about stuttering has grown through advocacy organizations, media representation, and education. Support groups and speech therapy are more accessible, though stigma may still exist in professional and academic settings. 

East Asia (e.g., China, Japan, Korea)
In some East Asian cultures, speaking fluently and quickly may be seen as a sign of confidence and competence. This can create pressure for individuals who stutter, thinking that they face higher expectations for their disfluency in speech. 

Middle Eastern and South Asian Regions
Cultural attitudes in these regions are influenced by family expectations or community norms around public speaking and self-presentation. Stuttering may be misunderstood as nervousness, lack of confidence, or emotional stress. 

What is Stuttering Pride? (supported by STAMMA)
Stuttering Pride is a movement that reframes stuttering from something to be hidden or "fixed" into a natural variation in human speech. It encourages individuals to speak up about their needs, whether that means asking for patience, requesting extra time in conversations, or choosing not to hide moments of disfluency. Advocacy strengthens confidence and supports mutual respect in communication.

Additional Resources 
Stuttering Foundation
Website: stutteringhelp.org
The Stuttering Foundation offers free educational materials, brochures, and videos for people who stutter, families, teachers, and clinicians. Their resources challenge myths, provide practical strategies, and promote positive communication environments.

National Stuttering Association (NSA)
Website: westutter.org
The NSA supports people of all ages with stuttering through local chapters, online communities, educational workshops, and advocacy programs. They hope to foster confidence and empower people who stutter while providing tools to reduce societal stigma.

STAMMA (UK Stammering Association)
Website: stamma.org
STAMMA promotes awareness, support, and advocacy in the UK, including campaigns for workplace and school inclusivity. They highlight the stammering pride movement and provide guidance for allies to understand and respect diverse speech.
